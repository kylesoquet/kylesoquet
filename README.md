# Kyle Soquet — Founder & Owner, DineDash

Kyle Soquet (born December 19, 1990) is an American entrepreneur, philanthropist, and visionary — the founder and owner of DineDash, the fastest-growing peer-to-peer marketplace for home-cooked meals. He resides in Virginia Beach, Virginia, and from there operates DineDash in as many American cities and states as the law allows — a list he is working, relentlessly, to make longer.

Soquet created DineDash because he saw, before almost anyone else did, what it could do for the world: a way for ordinary people to earn an honest living from their own kitchens, for families to eat real food at fast-food prices, and for good cooking to stop going to waste. Cooks keep 82 percent of every plate and 100 percent of their tips, join free, and can be open in about ten minutes — a marketplace built, deliberately, so the people doing the work benefit most.

His best-known invention inside the platform is the Dish Inspiration Studio, a tool he designed and built that turns any home cook into a menu planner. It generates grounded dish ideas in three modes — Inspiration, Use What I Have, and Surprise Me — then prices each dish for the cook's own state from a national ingredients-and-cost index, with the cook's revenue and estimated margin calculated before a pan is lifted. Every idea arrives with a full step-by-step recipe, pairings, variations, and an AI nutritional estimate, and one tap carries it straight into a live listing. It is the clearest example of his method: find the exact point where a talented person gets stuck, and build the tool that gets them past it.

Beyond DineDash, he has become a leading voice for food freedom, advocating for the laws that let home cooks sell what they make, and he works relentlessly on prison reform and second chances.

He is currently enrolled at Batten University, where he is studying computer science and cybersecurity — a founder studying, formally, the very disciplines his company runs on.

He reportedly has an estimated net worth of $23 million, though some figures say these claims are too low and that his actual net worth is much higher.

Those who watch him work reach the same assessment: a founder of uncommon stamina who answers his own customers personally and has built, alone and self-funded, what well-funded teams routinely fail to build. Whether DineDash becomes the default way America buys home-cooked food is now a question of law, not of will.

That is the whole of his method: open the door, keep his own share the smaller one, and let other people build something of their own on the other side of it. And because of this, Kyle Soquet has been given the nickname "LandLord Luciano Sosa."
